Frequently asked

Questions & answers

Here we've gathered the questions we get most often, organised by topic.

The purchase

About buying a kitten

How does registering interest and reservation work?

You're warmly welcome to get in touch if you're interested in a kitten. We're happy to tell you more about the kittens' personalities and help you find a cat that suits you. Once you've decided, the kitten is reserved through a reservation agreement.

When can the kittens move to their new home?

Our kittens move at 14 weeks of age at the earliest. By then they're vet-checked, vaccinated, microchipped and registered with SVERAK with a pedigree. They've also had a secure start together with their mother and littermates.

Can I come and see the kittens?

Yes. Once the kittens are old enough, we're happy to arrange a visit. You'll get to meet the kittens in a relaxed setting, get to know their personalities and ask any questions you have before deciding.

What's included in the price of a kitten?

The price includes neutering/spaying before delivery, age-appropriate vaccinations, microchipping, a veterinary health check, pedigree, hidden defect insurance, a kitten starter kit and lifelong support. Read more under Purchase Info.

The breeds

About the breeds

Which breeds do you breed?

We breed Ragdoll and British Shorthair/Longhair, in exciting colours such as cinnamon, chocolate and lilac. The focus of our breeding work is always on health and temperament, rather than colour and pattern.

What does "PG-free" mean?

PG refers to Patriarca Gucci, a Ragdoll ancestor linked to hereditary dwarfism when she appears on both sides of a pedigree. "PG-free" means Patriarca Gucci doesn't appear in the checked generations of the pedigree — it's a pedigree review, not a DNA test.

How do Ragdoll and British Shorthair/Longhair differ?

The Ragdoll is large, soft and trusting, known for its calm temperament and deep blue eyes. British Shorthair/Longhair are steady, calm cats with round features and a dense, plush coat. Both breeds thrive in a family environment.

Everyday life

About everyday life with the cat

How are the kittens socialised?

Our kittens grow up in a home environment and get used to people, everyday sounds and daily handling early on. Whether they grow up with us or with one of our foster homes, they get plenty of closeness, play and care every day, giving them a great foundation to become confident, social family cats.

Do your cats go outside?

Yes, some of our neutered family cats get to enjoy time outdoors in a safe setting. Our fertile breeding cats, on the other hand, don't roam freely outdoors — they're kept as indoor cats or in a secure outdoor enclosure during their breeding years. The cat's safety always comes first for us.

What should I think about before bringing the kitten home?

We recommend continuing with the same food and litter the kitten is used to for the first while, and giving the kitten time to explore its new home at its own pace. Read more in our Bringing Home guide.

Health

About our health work

How do you approach health in your breeding?

Every pairing is carefully planned with a focus on health, temperament and breed-typical looks. We use DNA tests, blood type tests and regular heart screenings to give our kittens the best possible foundation for good health.

What does the hidden defect insurance cover?

This insurance gives you extra protection against congenital or hereditary defects that couldn't be detected at the pre-delivery veterinary check. In addition, we recommend taking out your own insurance for the kitten as soon as possible.

Are the kittens vaccinated and microchipped before delivery?

Yes. All kittens are vaccinated according to age, microchipped and vet-checked before moving to their new home, at 14 weeks of age at the earliest.

Breeding

About our breeding work

Why do you use foster homes?

We believe fertile cats do best growing up and living in small family groups, with plenty of time, calm and individual care. That's why our breeding cats live with carefully chosen foster homes from kittenhood. This gives the cats a secure family life while allowing us to carry out responsible breeding with their wellbeing at the centre.

Can I buy a kitten for breeding?

Yes, we occasionally sell kittens for breeding. We carefully select which cats are suitable for breeding, and we'd like to get to know you and your goals as a breeder before we make a decision together.

How do you choose which cats are used for breeding?

Every combination is carefully planned with a focus on health, temperament and breed type. We use health tests, DNA analyses and pedigrees as part of our breeding work to give the kittens the best possible start.

What's the price difference between a companion cat and a breeding cat?

A companion cat costs 14,000 SEK and is sold neutered/spayed. Breeding cats are sold only to carefully selected breeders, and price and terms vary depending on the cat's quality, health, lines and breeding potential.
